## What to Evaluate

Assess whether the content provides structured comparisons — tables, side-by-side analyses, or systematic pro/con evaluations — that AI engines can directly extract and present when users ask "X vs Y" or "which is better" queries.

## Scoring Criteria

- **Comparison tables** (0-8 points): Tables that explicitly compare two or more items across multiple dimensions. Award more for tables with clear headers, consistent criteria, and balanced coverage.
- **Structured pros/cons** (0-4 points): Explicit "Pros" and "Cons" sections, advantage/disadvantage lists, or "strengths and weaknesses" breakdowns.
- **Comparative language** (0-3 points): Direct comparative analysis ("X is faster but Y is more affordable", "Unlike X, Y provides...") — but only award if it adds real analytical value, not just incidental use of comparison words.

## High Score Examples

- A hosting providers page with a feature comparison matrix (price, storage, support, uptime)
- "Pros and Cons of Remote Work" with bulleted, balanced lists
- Side-by-side analysis: "React vs Vue: When to Choose Each"

## Low Score Indicators

- No comparison elements despite comparing things in prose
- Incidental use of words like "better" or "versus" without actual comparative structure
- One-sided comparison that only presents one option favorably

## Context Sensitivity

- Not all content needs comparisons — a company's "About Us" page is not a comparison. Award 0 points without penalty for non-comparative content types.
- Product/service pages benefit from "how we compare" sections
- Tutorial content comparing approaches or tools should score high
- Don't award points for incidental comparison words in non-comparative content
